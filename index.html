Humanoid Standing Balance & 100N Push Recovery

By Jeevanand Maurya (BTech CSE-AI, Lucknow University) · Website · GitHub · LinkedIn · Repository

This is a write-up of my project on training a 17-actuator bipedal humanoid to maintain stationary balance and recover from unexpected horizontal pushes. I trained the agent using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and iterated across three distinct reward phases (v1 to v3). The best performing model is v3 (origin-locked balance, 50M steps).

Model Card: Humanoid-Balance-v3 (Best)

System specifications, observation space topology, curriculum disturbance schedule, and operational limits of the trained balance policy.

Model Details · PPO Actor-Critic
Architecture Continuous Actor-Critic MLP. The actor network has 2 hidden layers with 64 units each and Tanh activations. Outputs continuous torque values for 17 actuated joints, bound to [-0.4, 0.4].
Task Maintain stationary upright bipedal stance, lock root position near origin, and absorb random horizontal impulses up to 100N without stepping or falling.
Observation Space (367-D)
  • Base MuJoCo Kinematics (348-D): 22 joint positions, 23 joint velocities, 140 body inertia terms, 84 body velocities, 17 actuator reaction forces, and 84 external contact forces.
  • Disturbance Force (2-D): Immediate horizontal push vector $(F_x, F_y)$.
  • Previous Action (17-D): Control vector from step $t-1$ to enforce action smoothness.
Disturbance Curriculum
  • v1 Baseline (0N): No pushes — basic standing balance only.
  • v2 (50N): Light 50N pushes, spaced 200 simulation steps apart.
  • v3 Best (100N): Pushes up to 100N with origin-locked drift penalty.
Training Setup Gymnasium Humanoid-v5 with MuJoCo 3.12, Stable-Baselines3 PPO, and 12 parallel environments (SubprocVecEnv). Trained locally on an RTX 3050 Laptop GPU / AMD Ryzen 5.
Failure Condition Termination triggers if torso center-of-mass height drops below $z < 0.8\text{m}$ (nominal height is $1.4\text{m}$) or torso tilt exceeds $60^\circ$ after a 100-step grace period.

How I Improved It: From v1 to v3

Getting standing balance to work took several distinct iterations to solve joint chattering, lateral drifting, and fall recovery.

v1: Baseline Locomotion Wrapper Fell in ~74 steps
The default Gymnasium Humanoid reward is built for running forward. In v1, I zeroed the forward reward and added an alive bonus. But without temporal action damping, the actuators oscillated violently and the robot collapsed under even a light 50N push.
Observation: 350-D · Mean Episode Duration: 63.6 steps · Reward: ~531
v2: Action Buffer & Smoothness Penalty First 1,000-Step Survival
I appended the previous action vector $a_{t-1}$ to the observation space (expanding it to 367-D) and introduced a temporal action change penalty: $-0.02 \sum |a_t - a_{t-1}|$. This cut down high-frequency flutter and allowed the policy to survive light pushes for full 1,000-step episodes.
Observation: 367-D · Mean Episode Duration: 1000.0 steps · Reward: ~10,195
v3: Origin Anchoring & Drift Cost Best Model
In v2, the robot learned to skate laterally to absorb pushes rather than standing its ground. In v3, I added a quadratic drift penalty: $-5.0 \cdot \|p_{\text{torso}, xy} - p_{\text{target}, xy}\|^2$, forcing the robot to push back toward its starting position.
Observation: 367-D · Mean Episode Duration: 1000.0 steps · Reward: ~10,171

Reward Formulation (v3 Best)

The step reward $R_t$ balances posture stability, energy efficiency, and impulse recovery:

$$\begin{aligned} R_t = \; & w_{\text{alive}} \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{upright}) \\ & + w_{\text{upright}} \cdot (z_{\text{torso}} \cdot z_{\text{up}}) \\ & + w_{\text{height}} \cdot \exp\left(-10.0 \, (z_{\text{torso}} - 1.4)^2\right) \\ & + w_{\text{feet}} \cdot \exp\left(-40.0 \, \big((z_{\text{rfoot}} - 0.17)^2 + (z_{\text{lfoot}} - 0.17)^2\big)\right) \\ & - w_{\text{vel}} \cdot \|v_{\text{root}}\|^2 - w_{\text{ang}} \cdot \|\omega_{\text{root}}\|^2 \\ & - w_{\text{smooth}} \cdot \|a_t - a_{t-1}\|_1 - w_{\text{drift}} \cdot \|p_{\text{torso}, xy} - p_{\text{target}, xy}\|^2 \\ & + w_{\text{posture}} \cdot \exp\left(-3.0 \, \text{MSE}(q_{\text{joints}}, q_{\text{nominal}})\right) \\ & - w_{\text{jointvel}} \cdot \text{MSE}(\dot{q}_{\text{joints}}, 0) \end{aligned}$$
